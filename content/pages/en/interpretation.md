# Interpretation Services for Spoken-Language Assignments

**Short answer:** Globalize lists interpretation among its services. To request an assessment, share the languages involved, assignment date, setting, subject, and the support you are looking for. Any proposed arrangement is subject to review of the specific request; this page does not promise a particular mode, language pair, location, equipment, or booking availability.

## Start with the assignment details

Interpretation supports spoken communication between people using different languages. It is different from translating a written document. A useful first enquiry explains what the communication is for and what conditions the interpreter would need to understand.

## Information to include in your enquiry

- Date, start/end time, and time zone
- Source and target languages, including the direction needed
- Meeting or event purpose and subject matter
- Expected participant or audience size, if known
- Location or online platform, if already selected
- Available agenda, presentation materials, or terminology references
- Any accessibility, confidentiality, recording, or technical requirements that need to be discussed

These are scoping details, not a promise that every requested setting or requirement can be accommodated. Do not send confidential materials until the appropriate sharing and handling arrangements have been confirmed.

## Interpretation is not written translation

An interpretation enquiry concerns spoken communication. If you need a contract, certificate, report, presentation, or other written file translated, use the relevant written-translation service route instead. If you need both services for an event, describe each separately so the request can be assessed accurately.

## Distinguish the assignment setting

The same language pair can involve different conditions. A short bilateral conversation, a multi-speaker meeting, a public event, and a remote call may place different demands on turn-taking, sound, preparation, and participant access. Describe the actual setting rather than naming a mode you are unsure about.

| Assignment detail | Why it helps scope the request |
|---|---|
| Speakers and listeners | Clarifies who needs to communicate and in which direction |
| Agenda and subject | Shows terminology and preparation needs |
| Venue or platform | Identifies the environment to assess, without promising technical support |
| Schedule and session length | Helps distinguish the requested time window and breaks |
| Materials and access limits | Shows what preparation material exists and how it may be shared |

If the event changes after the enquiry—for example, its date, language direction, participant count, or platform—update the request before relying on any earlier proposal. Confirm the final scope and responsibilities in writing. This is practical planning guidance; Globalize’s actual interpreter availability and arrangements must be confirmed for the individual assignment.

## Plan an interpretation assignment

A clear brief helps the provider assess the people, subject, setting, and language needs involved. Include the event purpose, date and time, duration, venue or remote context, agenda, number of participants, speaker order if known, and the languages expected. Identify whether the work concerns a conference, meeting, interview, site visit, training session, or another format. These details describe the request; they do not by themselves confirm a particular mode, team size, equipment package, or geographic availability.

Send names, titles, organization names, specialist terms, slides, and relevant background materials in advance when they may affect meaning. Mark any information that is confidential and confirm the handling arrangement before sending it. If speakers will use prepared remarks, explain whether they will read the script exactly or depart from it. Last-minute changes can affect terminology and context, so identify who will circulate updates and who can answer questions during the assignment.

| Planning item | Details to provide |
|---|---|
| Event | Purpose, format, subject, date, duration, and location or platform |
| Languages | Source and target languages, including any changes during the event |
| Participants | Approximate number, speaker roles, and whether questions or discussion are expected |
| Materials | Agenda, slides, names, glossary, scripts, and technical terms |
| Delivery | Requested format and any confirmed venue or platform constraints |

## Confirm roles, materials, and contingencies

Ask what the quotation includes and what remains the client’s responsibility. Depending on the confirmed scope, this may include preparing slides, sharing links, scheduling a sound check, arranging room access, or distributing receiver equipment. Do not assume that a listed service includes recording, transcription, translation of slides, event moderation, technical production, or interpretation equipment unless it is specifically included in writing.

Agree how participants will request clarification, how breaks and schedule changes will be communicated, and who resolves a source-language ambiguity. Provide a point of contact who can explain names, acronyms, and specialized references without interrupting the event. For remote work, confirm the platform, audio setup, access permissions, and backup contact before the session. For on-site work, confirm access and any venue rules that affect setup.

After the assignment, distinguish interpretation from a written transcript or certified translation. If a record is needed, specify it separately and confirm whether it is available. The client remains responsible for the accuracy of source statements and for decisions made by participants; interpretation does not certify the underlying information or guarantee an event outcome.

## Review the assignment before confirmation

Before accepting a proposed arrangement, check that the estimate identifies the requested languages, duration, location or platform, materials, preparation assumptions, and deliverables. Ask how schedule changes, cancellations, additional sessions, or a change in subject will affect the scope. If a technical setup is involved, identify which party supplies and tests each component and who is responsible for resolving access or audio problems. Put these decisions in writing so event staff and the language team work from the same plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### What information should I provide for an interpretation enquiry?

Include the languages and direction, date and time zone, assignment purpose and subject, expected audience, setting or platform, and any agenda or materials available. Note requirements that need discussion, such as accessibility, confidentiality, recording, or technology.

### Does Globalize confirm every interpretation mode or language pair?

No. The specific mode, languages, location, and availability must be assessed for the request. The general service listing does not confirm every combination.

### Can an interpretation booking include equipment or technical support?

This page does not confirm equipment supply or technical support. Include your venue or platform and technical requirements in the enquiry so they can be assessed; do not assume that equipment is included.

### Is interpretation the same as translating a document?

No. Interpretation concerns spoken communication; translation concerns written content. Describe the service you need, or list both separately if your request includes spoken and written work.
