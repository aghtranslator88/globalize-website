# Document Attestation and Legalisation: Check the Issuing Authority

**Short answer:** Attestation and legalisation procedures depend on where a document was issued, its type, and which authority or recipient requires it. Start with the official source for the issuing or receiving authority. The examples below apply to different jurisdictions and must not be combined into one universal checklist.

## Official procedure examples

| Authority and official source | Scope of guidance | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| [Egypt Ministry of Foreign Affairs: attestation-office procedures](https://www.mfa.gov.eg/ar/RatificationOffices/AuthenticationProcedure) | The Ministry lists prerequisite approvals by Egyptian issuing body and document class, with separate notes about documents issued outside Egypt and translations. | The listed sequences are not one process for every Egyptian document, foreign authority, or receiving country. Check the relevant category and confirm current requirements. |
| [UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs: attestation of official documents and certificates](https://www.mofa.gov.ae/en/Services/attestation) | The UAE Ministry’s official service page for attestation of official documents and certificates. | This is a UAE Ministry service, not an Egyptian or UK procedure and not a Globalize accreditation, partnership, or acceptance statement. Confirm eligibility and current steps on the source page. |
| [UK Legalisation Office: get a document legalised](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised/overview) | UK guidance for certain official documents issued in the UK and submitted to its Legalisation Office; the page excludes documents issued outside the UK from this service. | This is not a process for Egyptian- or UAE-issued documents and does not establish a universal legalisation sequence. |

## Keep the document route and translation request separate

The authority responsible for a document’s authentication or legalisation determines its own process. Globalize’s role is translation: it can assess a translation request when you provide the document and the recipient’s written instructions. Translation does not replace an authority’s authentication, legalisation, apostille, or approval step.

Verify the issuing country, document type, required prior approvals, order of steps, whether translation is required, and any specified translator statement, stamp, signature, or format. Do not rely on a general list if your document or transaction is not explicitly covered.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there one attestation sequence that applies to every document?

No. Official instructions vary by authority, issuing country, and document class. Egypt’s and the UAE’s Ministries of Foreign Affairs and the UK Legalisation Office describe separate services; do not merge them into one checklist.

### Can the UK Legalisation Office legalise a document issued in Egypt?

The linked GOV.UK guidance says documents issued outside the UK cannot be legalised through that service and directs users to the country of issue. Check with the relevant issuing-country authority.

### Does translation replace attestation or legalisation?

No. Translation and an authority’s authentication or legalisation are distinct steps. Follow the official procedure for the document and ask the recipient what translation format it requires.

### What should I share when requesting a translation assessment?

Provide a complete copy, source and target languages, issuing country, recipient and purpose, requested deadline, and written certification or formatting requirements. The authority—not the translator—determines whether its attestation requirements are met.
