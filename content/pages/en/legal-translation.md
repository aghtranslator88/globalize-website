# Legal Document Translation Services

> **Quick answer:** Legal translation services transfer the text of a contract, court record, or business document into another language while preserving its clauses, names, dates, figures, and terminology. Globalize reviews the file, purpose, and recipient to define the translation scope, review needs, and any requested certification elements. The service is linguistic, not legal advice, and an external recipient—not the translator—decides whether to accept a document.

A misplaced negation, inconsistent defined term, or incorrect cross-reference can make a translated document difficult to use. Legal files therefore need more than general fluency: translators must track parties, obligations, conditions, exceptions, dates, amounts, and references throughout the document. Ambiguity in the source should be flagged rather than silently resolved.

## Legal documents we can assess

Requests may include contracts and agreements, powers of attorney and declarations, legal correspondence, pleadings and supporting records, judgments or decisions submitted for translation, and company documents such as formation records, board minutes, or commercial-register extracts. Document names and legal forms vary across jurisdictions. Share the title shown on the source instead of assuming that a familiar label means the same thing everywhere.

Whether a file is suitable for the service—and whether a stamp, signature, or accuracy statement is requested—depends on the actual document, language pair, and receiving authority’s instructions. Examples on this page are not a guarantee that every format or separate procedure is automatically included.

## What a careful legal translation checks

| Element | Translation focus |
|---|---|
| Parties and defined terms | Names, roles, definitions, and consistent use throughout the file |
| Rights and obligations | Negation, conditions, exceptions, duration, and remedies as written—without added interpretation |
| Dates and figures | Amounts, percentages, article numbers, references, and annex cross-links |
| Structure | Headings, numbering, tables, signatures, stamps, and visible annotations |
| Terminology | Context-appropriate, consistent equivalents; flag ambiguity instead of deciding it for the client |

If a scan is unclear or a source abbreviation has more than one possible meaning, the translator may need a clarification. A translation should not silently correct an inconsistency or change a clause’s apparent effect without documented client direction.

## Translation is not legal advice

Translation conveys what the document says. It does not determine whether an agreement is valid or enforceable, assess a party’s rights, predict litigation, or interpret the governing law. For legal analysis, drafting, or review, consult a lawyer qualified in the relevant jurisdiction. You can share the lawyer’s instructions or preferred terminology so they can be followed consistently in the language work.

Translation is also distinct from notarization, authentication, attestation, or consular legalization. If a recipient asks for a particular stamp, signature, translator statement, or separate procedure, send those instructions before work begins. A translation does not guarantee acceptance or replace a step required by another authority.

## How to prepare a legal translation request

1. Send a complete, legible copy, including available pages, annexes, stamps, and notes.
2. Identify the source and target languages and, if known, the document’s issuing country.
3. Name the recipient, purpose, and requested deadline; include written instructions.
4. Flag spellings or terminology that must match an earlier document or client glossary.
5. If the file is sensitive, ask about an NDA before sharing it; confidentiality terms can be discussed on request.

After reviewing the file, Globalize can define the scope and provide a request-specific quotation and schedule. Do not rely on a generic price or turnaround before the volume, complexity, and language pair have been assessed.

## Prepare legal source material for translation

Before requesting a quotation, identify the complete document set and the language direction. Include schedules, exhibits, signature pages, seals, handwritten additions, and relevant reverse sides. If a document refers to defined terms or an earlier agreement, provide the referenced material when it is available and authorized for sharing. Mark the controlling version so an outdated draft is not translated by mistake.

Names, dates, registration details, monetary amounts, article numbers, and defined terms deserve particular attention. Supply approved spellings and any client terminology list, while making clear whether it is binding or only a reference. Do not alter the source text to resolve a discrepancy; flag the issue and ask the document owner or legal adviser to clarify it.

## Keep translation separate from legal review

A translation conveys the source content in another language. It does not determine a party’s rights, validate a signature, verify the legal effect of a clause, or advise whether a document complies with a law. If the recipient needs legal analysis, obtain it from a qualified legal professional in the relevant jurisdiction. If the recipient has written translation or certification instructions, provide the exact current instruction with the request.

## Check the deliverable against the agreed scope

When the translated file is returned, compare the document list and page sequence with the quotation. Check names, dates, amounts, identifiers, headings, tables, footnotes, and referenced exhibits against the source. Report a suspected transcription or translation issue with the page or clause number and a marked source excerpt. A change to the source after translation may require a new scope assessment.

Ask in advance whether the requested output includes a particular layout, editable file, certification statement, signature, or company stamp. The supplied information confirms the service category but does not define every project’s format, review depth, certification wording, price, deadline, or separate notarization/legalization steps; those details must be established for the individual quotation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Globalize provide legal advice with translation?

No. The service transfers text between languages; it does not advise on rights, interpret law, or assess whether a contract is valid. Consult a qualified lawyer and, if useful, share their written instructions for the translation.

### What kinds of legal documents can I submit?

You can request an assessment for contracts, powers of attorney, correspondence, court documents, business records, and other legal materials. The actual file, language pair, and purpose determine whether the request fits the service.

### Does a translation guarantee acceptance by a court or embassy?

No. Requirements vary by recipient and procedure, and the receiving authority makes its own decision. Share its current instructions so the translation scope can be set without promising an external outcome.

### Can I ask about an NDA before sharing a sensitive file?

Yes. Ask about a non-disclosure agreement before sending the document. You can make an initial enquiry without attaching information you do not wish to share before the handling terms are discussed.
