# Medical Translation for Reports and Healthcare Records

> **Quick answer:** Medical translation services transfer reports, laboratory results, and healthcare records into another language while preserving the names, dates, figures, values, and units shown in the source. Globalize reviews the file, language pair, and recipient to scope the work, review, and any requested certification elements. Translation is not diagnosis or treatment advice: it does not interpret results or change the report’s content.

Healthcare documents contain specialized terms, abbreviations, and numerical data. A translator should not guess at an unclear value or silently “correct” a result to make it appear plausible. If a scan is incomplete or records conflict, the unclear point should be flagged and a clearer copy or client clarification requested.

## Medical files we can assess

Requests may include medical reports and clinician letters, discharge summaries, laboratory and pathology results, imaging reports, operative records, medication lists or written instructions, and healthcare or research materials requiring language transfer. Organizations may also ask about specialized medical or pharmaceutical content; the file, audience, and language pair determine whether the request fits the service.

An example list does not mean that a recipient requires or will accept a document, or that every file follows the same workflow. Share the document title as printed and its intended use. For an initial enquiry, avoid sending personal details that are not needed to assess the request.

## Information a careful translation preserves

| Source element | Translation handling |
|---|---|
| Patient or facility name | Match the spelling supplied by the client or specified identity record when relevant |
| Date and time | Carry over the displayed format and time reference without inferring another date |
| Results and figures | Preserve values, decimal marks, signs, and reference markers as shown |
| Units | Retain the displayed unit; do not convert or substitute it without documented instruction |
| Abbreviations and notes | Use a clear equivalent where available and flag ambiguity rather than guess |
| Layout and stamps | Preserve relevant headings, structure, margins, and legible source elements |

If the recipient requests the original imaging file, confirm that it is included; translating a written radiology report is not the same as translating or interpreting the images themselves.

## Medical translation is not medical interpretation

The language team translates what the record states. It does not diagnose a condition, recommend treatment, interpret a test result, or decide whether a procedure is appropriate. Clinical questions belong with the treating clinician or healthcare provider. A dose, unit, or unclear phrase should never be changed merely to make it sound more plausible.

A recipient may specify a certification statement, signature, or format. Send current written instructions before work begins so the requested scope can be assessed. Translation does not guarantee acceptance by a hospital, insurer, embassy, or other organization, and this page does not determine what records are needed for treatment or travel.

## How to prepare a medical translation request

1. Send a clear, complete copy of the pages to be translated.
2. State the source and target languages and the recipient, if known.
3. Include written formatting or certification instructions and the requested deadline.
4. Flag important name spellings and whether they must match a passport or another record.
5. If you need an NDA, ask before sharing the file; confidentiality terms can be discussed for projects that require them.

After reviewing the file, Globalize can provide a request-specific scope, quotation, and schedule. No urgent turnaround or single format is assumed; language, volume, image quality, and requested formatting affect the assessment.

## Prepare clinical records without changing their meaning

Send the complete pages that belong to the request, including relevant tables, units, reference ranges, abbreviations, handwritten notes, stamps, and attachments. Use a legible scan and keep the page order. Identify cropped or unreadable areas instead of guessing what they say. If the source contains a correction or multiple versions, identify which copy controls and preserve the original wording for review.

Provide the source and target languages, intended reader, and purpose of the translation. A patient, clinician, insurer, university, or administrative recipient may need different terminology or presentation. Share a glossary or prior approved translation only when it is relevant, current, and authorized. Spellings of names and dates should follow the supplied identity or record documents; do not silently reconcile a conflict between records.

## Distinguish language work from clinical judgment

Translation does not diagnose a condition, interpret a test result, confirm a treatment, verify a dosage, or replace a clinician’s explanation. A translation provider should not be presented as the source of medical advice. If a value, abbreviation, or handwritten entry is unclear, ask the record owner or treating professional to clarify it rather than infer a clinical meaning.

## Review the delivered file against the source

Check the names, dates, units, decimal points, medication names, headings, tables, and sequence of pages. Confirm that any requested layout or editable format was included in the agreed scope. Report a possible omission or transcription issue by page and location, attaching the corresponding source excerpt. Do not use a translation to make care decisions when a qualified clinician needs to interpret the underlying record.

Before sharing sensitive health information, read the provider’s current privacy terms and use its stated submission channel. The information supplied for this project does not establish a particular storage, access, retention, deletion, clinical-review, certification, price, or delivery-time policy. Ask about the arrangements required for your project before transferring complete records.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does medical translation diagnose or interpret test results?

No. It transfers what appears in the source and does not interpret findings, diagnose a condition, or recommend treatment. Ask the treating clinician or a qualified healthcare professional about clinical meaning.

### Can you translate a report or test result that is unclear?

An unreadable section may require a clearer copy or a client clarification. A translator should not guess a number or abbreviation or alter a result based on a medical expectation.

### Does translation guarantee acceptance by a hospital or embassy?

No. Requirements depend on the institution and purpose, and the recipient decides what it accepts. Share its instructions so the requested translation format can be assessed without promising an external outcome.

### What information should I send for a quotation?

Send complete pages, the source and target languages, recipient and purpose, requested deadline, and any written formatting or certification instructions. An NDA can be discussed before a sensitive file is shared.
