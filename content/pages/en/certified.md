# Certified Translation Services for Official Documents

> **Answer Box:** Certified translation is a professional translation of an official document that is checked against the source and issued with the appropriate certification elements, such as a company stamp, authorized signature, or accuracy statement where applicable. The required format depends on the document, target language, purpose, and receiving authority’s instructions.

When a certificate, contract, record, or official file must be submitted in another language, names, dates, identification numbers, stamps, annotations, and document structure all matter. Globalize provides certified translation for individuals, companies, law firms, and institutions, with the scope assessed before a quotation is issued.

“Certified” does not mean that one format works for every recipient. Authorities may request different elements and distinguish translation from notarization, authentication, attestation, or legalization. A reliable enquiry begins by identifying the recipient and its instructions.

## What is certified translation?

Certified translation is a translated version of a document prepared for official or institutional use and issued with the certification elements appropriate to its intended purpose. Depending on the request, those elements may include the Globalize company stamp, an authorized signature, and a certificate or statement confirming translation accuracy.

The source and translation must be checked together, the order of information kept clear, and illegible entries or handwritten notes identified where necessary. Names and numbers require close attention. Supply any prescribed translator declaration before work begins.

### How do translation, certification, notarization, and legalization differ?

| Term | Main function | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | Transfers the document’s content into the requested language | Language, completeness, and source legibility |
| Certified translation | Adds the appropriate certification elements, such as a stamp, signature, or accuracy statement | The format required by the recipient |
| Notarization | Verifies a signature or capacity under the applicable procedure | Whether it is required and which authority performs it |
| Authentication or legalization | Confirms a document for use in another administrative or national context | The sequence, destination country, and receiving authority |
| Final acceptance | A decision made by the organization receiving the file | It cannot be controlled or guaranteed by the translation provider |

These terms are not interchangeable. A transaction may require translation alone or another step before or after it. Provide the recipient’s instructions so the scope can be identified accurately.

## Which documents may need certified translation?

Common requests include birth, marriage, and death certificates, police records, movement certificates, academic records, contracts, powers of attorney, judgments, legal correspondence, company records, commercial registers, and financial or administrative documents.

Supported categories do not share one set of recipient rules, and similarly named documents are not necessarily interchangeable. Use the exact document title and identify its issuing country and intended recipient.

Specialized material requires subject knowledge. The translator, reviewer, terminology resources, and workflow should match the file, language pair, and purpose—not merely the fact that a stamp will be added.

## Why do receiving-authority requirements vary?

Each authority controls its procedure. It may request an accuracy statement, translator competence, a date, contact details, or a defined translator status. Requirements may differ between application categories in the same country.

Official guidance illustrates the variation. A defined U.S. immigrant-visa process asks for a signed accuracy and competence statement. UK visitor guidance lists accuracy confirmation, date, name, signature, and contact details. Canadian guidance distinguishes certified-translator work from situations requiring an affidavit. These examples are not universal rules.

Do not rely on another applicant’s experience, an outdated checklist, or “accepted everywhere” wording. Read the exact procedure, send it with the document, and ask the recipient when instructions are unclear.

## How should you prepare a certified translation request?

### 1. Identify the purpose and recipient

Name the embassy, government office, university, court, employer, or institution. Include the country, application type, and official requirements link when available.

### 2. Confirm the source and target languages

Specify the source language and the recipient’s requested language. Identify names that must match a passport or another identity record.

### 3. Provide a complete, legible copy

Every page, stamp, signature, reverse-side entry, and margin note should be visible. Do not crop edges or alter an image in a way that hides marks or changes numbers.

### 4. Send the certification instructions

Send the official wording for any required stamp, signature, accuracy statement, notarization, or other step. A separate procedure is not automatically included.

### 5. Review the quotation and scope

Pricing may depend on volume, language pair, specialization, source quality, formatting, deadline, and confirmed additional steps. Standard-document rates are not universal project pricing.

## Common mistakes that can delay a file

- requesting translation without identifying the recipient or procedure;
- sending a cropped, blurred, or incomplete multi-page document;
- assuming a stamp replaces notarization or legalization when the recipient requires another step;
- using a name spelling that differs from the passport;
- relying on old instructions or an unofficial social-media post;
- assuming one turnaround or price applies to every document;
- treating document-format acceptance as a guarantee of a visa, admission, court, or other outcome.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Globalize certified translation accepted by every embassy?

No single format is guaranteed for every embassy or recipient. Requirements vary by country, application, document, and language. Check the receiving authority’s current instructions and include them with the request; the authority retains the final acceptance decision.

### Does certified translation include notarization or consular legalization?

Not automatically. Translation, notarization, authentication, attestation, and legalization are different procedures. The quotation should state clearly which service is included and which step, if any, must be completed separately.

### What information is needed for a quotation?

Provide the complete document, source and target languages, recipient, country, purpose, requested deadline, and any certification or formatting instructions. These details allow the scope, price, and timing to be assessed instead of using a general estimate.

### Can an enquiry start with an electronic copy?

A clear copy may be sent through the agreed channel for initial assessment. Whether an electronic copy is sufficient for final production, or whether the original must be presented, depends on the document, receiving authority, and agreed delivery process.
