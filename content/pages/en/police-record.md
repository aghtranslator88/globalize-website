## Which document does this page cover?

The issuing body may call the document a police certificate, police record, criminal record, criminal-status certificate, or criminal-record check. Labels vary across jurisdictions, so a familiar English phrase should not replace the official source title automatically.

| Term | Editorial use on this page | What must be checked |
|---|---|---|
| Police certificate | Often used by immigration or government processes | Recipient wording and issuing jurisdiction |
| Police record | The preferred broad search label for this route | Whether it matches the actual document title |
| Criminal record | A common variant whose legal meaning may be broader or narrower | Source content and recipient context |
| Criminal record check | May describe a check, result, or certificate depending on the system | The function and form of the issued record |

The translation should not describe the holder as having “no criminal record” unless that conclusion is expressly stated by the source. A translator transfers the displayed content and result; the translator does not infer the holder’s legal status.

## What requires careful review?

Fields vary by issuer, but every visible item must be considered, including names, identity details, numbers, dates, issuing body, stated purpose, displayed result, annotations, stamps, signatures, and reference codes where present.

Fingerprints, photographs, security markings, and illegible areas should be described or marked without inventing missing information. Supply any reverse side containing entries.

Provide the passport spelling when the recipient expects a match, while preserving the source. Flag inconsistent spellings before issue rather than silently normalizing them.

## How should you prepare a police record translation request?

### 1. Send the complete document

Provide a clear scan or photograph of every page and relevant side. Edges, stamps, numbers, codes, and annotations should be visible without glare, blur, or cropping.

### 2. Identify the issuing country and authority

State the country and issuer because titles, layouts, languages, and administrative functions differ.

### 3. Name the recipient and purpose

Identify the embassy, immigration body, employer, university, court, government office, or other recipient when known. Send the official instructions or link instead of relying on another applicant’s experience.

### 4. Confirm the languages and name spelling

State the source and target languages and any spelling that must match a passport or identity record.

### 5. Separate translation from other procedures

A stamp or signature on a translation does not automatically amount to notarization, authentication, attestation, or legalization. If the recipient requests another procedure, send its instructions and ensure the quotation states whether that step is included or separate.

## Why must recipient instructions be checked?

There is no single rule for all destinations. [Official U.S. guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-5-collect-financial-evidence-and-other-supporting-documents/step-7-collect-civil-documents.html) for a defined immigrant-visa process links police-certificate requirements to age, residence periods, and country. [UK visitor guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/visitor-visa-guide-to-supporting-documents/guide-to-supporting-documents-visiting-the-uk) specifies translation details for documents outside English or Welsh. [Canadian guidance](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=039) for police certificates outside English or French requires a certified-translator translation.

These scoped examples only demonstrate variation. Consult the official source for the current application because rules can change.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- sending a cropped or unreadable image or omitting a relevant reverse side;
- describing a file only as a “police record” without its official title and issuing country;
- supplying a name spelling that differs from the passport without flagging it;
- assuming every recipient accepts the same format or uses one validity period;
- treating translation as a substitute for a separately required legalization procedure;
- relying on outdated guidance or another person’s application route;
- expecting translation to guarantee a visa, employment, admission, court, or administrative outcome.

## Frequently asked questions

### What information is needed for a police record translation?

Send a complete legible copy, issuing country and authority, source and target languages, recipient, purpose, requested deadline, and any official instructions or name spellings that must be matched.

### Do “police record,” “police certificate,” and “criminal record” always mean the same thing?

No. The terms can overlap, but their legal or administrative meaning varies by country and authority. Use the title printed on the record and the recipient’s terminology instead of assuming universal equivalence.

### Does a certified translation guarantee acceptance of the police record?

No. Globalize can prepare the translation with the agreed certification elements, but an embassy, immigration body, employer, university, court, or other recipient retains the final acceptance decision.

### Does police record translation include notarization or legalization?

Not automatically. Translation, notarization, authentication, attestation, and legalization are different procedures. Send the recipient’s instructions and confirm every included step in the quotation before work begins.
