Use this page when your document is titled “movement certificate” or the recipient asks for a translation under that name. Document titles and functions differ among countries and authorities. We do not assume that every movement certificate is a standardized entry-and-exit record or covers a particular period. The printed title, issuer, and recipient’s instructions matter.

## What does “movement certificate” mean for a translation request?

On this page, the term identifies a document the customer presents under that title or one confirmed by the recipient. Labels such as “travel history,” “entry/exit record,” or “travel history certificate” are not automatic equivalents in every system. They may be informal descriptions or refer to a different document.

Before work begins, provide an image of the document’s official title and identify the country and issuer. If the recipient uses a specific English or Arabic label, send that wording as it appears in the instructions. This helps preserve the document name without changing its meaning or implying that a different record is equivalent.

| Visible or requested detail | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Printed document title | Preserve it and provide the recipient’s requested label |
| Issuing authority or country | Supply visible information without inferring an unstated jurisdiction |
| Dates, reference numbers, or fields | Ensure they are legible and every page is included |
| Stamps, signatures, and annotations | Send clear images of marks, margins, and any relevant reverse side |
| Recipient terminology | Do not replace “movement certificate” with “travel history” or “entry/exit record” unless the document or recipient supports that term |

## What does the translation review cover?

The reviewer checks the visible content of the supplied document: its title, any holder name, issuing body, dates, reference numbers, tables or fields, stamps, signatures, and notes. The page does not assume that every form contains the same fields.

If part of the document is unclear, it should be flagged rather than guessed or completed from an outside source. Send all pages, including a reverse side that carries a stamp or annotation. If a name must match a passport, provide the requested spelling and flag any difference from the document.

Translation transfers the text and information shown; it does not verify actual travel movements, retrieve a record from a database, or determine whether dates prove entry, departure, or residence. Interpreting the legal effect of a certificate is outside this page’s scope.

## How should you prepare a movement-certificate request?

### 1. Send a complete legible copy

Make sure all pages, edges, numbers, stamps, and notes can be read. Avoid cropping, glare, or shadows that hide information. If a page or reverse side is blank, explain that rather than omitting it without context.

### 2. Give the official title and source

Provide the printed document title, issuing country, and authority when shown. Do not describe it only as “travel history” if the document has a different official title.

### 3. Identify the languages and recipient

State the document’s language, the requested language, recipient, destination country, and purpose when known. Send the official instructions; do not infer that the document is required merely because it relates to travel.

### 4. Confirm certification elements and separate procedures

If the recipient specifies a stamp, signature, or statement, include the exact wording. Appropriate certification elements depend on the request. Notarization, authentication, attestation, or legalization are distinct and are not automatically included.

### 5. Ask before handing over an original

An initial review can begin with a clear copy. Whether that copy is sufficient for production or an original must later be presented depends on the document, recipient instructions, and delivery process. Do not send an original before the procedure is agreed.

## Is a movement certificate required for every visa or application?

That should not be assumed. This review did not establish a current primary source requiring a movement certificate for all visas, embassies, or residence and employment applications. The presence of travel-related documents in one specific checklist does not prove that this particular certificate is required in another process.

Check the recipient’s instructions for the exact application. If a certificate is requested, confirm the accepted title, issuing country or authority, language, copy format, and any additional step. These details may vary and change; the recipient makes its own decision independently of the translation.

## Frequently asked questions

### What information is needed for a movement certificate translation?

Send a complete legible copy, the printed document title, issuing country and authority, source and target languages, recipient, purpose, requested deadline, and any certification or formatting instructions.

### Is a movement certificate the same as a travel-history or entry/exit record?

Not necessarily. Document names and functions vary by authority. Use the printed title and the recipient’s wording rather than treating these terms as automatic synonyms.

### Does translation interpret travel history or verify entries and exits?

No. Translation conveys the information visible in the document; it does not verify an actual travel record or infer a legal or administrative meaning from dates and fields.

### Is a movement certificate required or accepted by every embassy?

No universal rule should be assumed. Check the current instructions for the recipient and application; that organization decides what it requires and accepts.
