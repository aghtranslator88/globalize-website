# Birth Certificate Translation for Official Use

> **Answer Box:** To request a birth certificate translation, send a complete legible copy and identify its issuing country and authority, source language, target language, recipient, and purpose. Globalize reviews the visible names, dates, fields, and stamps, then defines the scope and appropriate certification elements for the request. Translation cannot guarantee acceptance or an outcome decided by an external organization.

Birth certificates vary in title, format, and recorded information by country and issuing authority. A sound translation starts with the actual document, not an assumption that every certificate follows one template. This page covers birth-certificate translation. A family register or another civil-status record must be identified by its own official title before the enquiry is routed.

## Which birth-certificate document needs translation?

Send the title printed on the document, its issuing country, and the issuing authority. It may be a paper certificate, a computerized record, an extract, or another civil-status format. These forms should not be presumed identical or suitable for the same recipient.

| Visible document detail | What to include with the request |
|---|---|
| Certificate or extract title | The official printed title and any wording specified by the recipient |
| Certificate holder’s details | Required name spelling, including any passport or identity-document match; flag discrepancies |
| Birth information | Date and place exactly as shown, without an unrequested conversion or correction |
| Parent or registration details | All visible names, fields, and record information, even if a field appears incomplete |
| Stamps, endorsements, or notes | Clear images of every mark and any reverse side containing entries |

A family register is not a birth certificate. If the document contains records for several family members, use the [Document Translation Guides](https://www.globalizetl.com/en/documents) or send its official title for correct routing. Do not use an online translated sample as an official template or a format guaranteed to be accepted.

## What should be checked in the translation?

Birth certificates require careful handling of names, dates, relationships, and civil-record details. Depending on what the source displays, a translator and reviewer may check:

- the holder’s full name, including order and spelling;
- date and place of birth, preserving the source without inferring an unstated calendar or format;
- parent names and fields describing relationships or registration;
- certificate or register number, issue date, and issuing authority;
- stamps, signatures, annotations, and handwritten additions;
- discrepancies or illegible areas that must be flagged rather than guessed.

Provide the approved name spelling if the recipient requires a match with a passport. If the certificate and identity document differ, flag the discrepancy. The translation should reflect the source and must not conceal or silently correct inconsistent data.

## How should you prepare a translation request?

### 1. Send a complete copy

Provide every page and any relevant reverse side. Keep edges, numbers, stamps, and annotations clear; avoid cropping, glare, and shadows that hide information.

### 2. Identify the issuing country and document

State the country and issuing authority. Say whether the record is computerized, an extract, or another format. If the recipient names a specific form, include that instruction rather than relying on the generic label “birth certificate.”

### 3. Name the recipient and target language

Specify the source and requested language, the recipient and country, and the purpose when known. Send the official instructions; do not assume that one embassy’s or university’s rules apply to another organization.

### 4. Share certification or formatting instructions

If the recipient specifies a statement, signature, stamp, paper copy, or another step, provide its exact wording. Certification elements are selected for the request; notarization or legalization is not automatically included.

### 5. Ask before sending an original

An initial review can begin with a clear electronic copy. Whether a copy is sufficient for production, or an original must later be presented, depends on the document, recipient instructions, and delivery process. Do not mail or hand over an original before confirming the appropriate procedure.

## Do birth-certificate requirements vary by recipient?

Yes. Instructions for one process should not be generalized. In a defined U.S. immigrant-visa process, the [U.S. Department of State](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-5-collect-financial-evidence-and-other-supporting-documents/step-7-collect-civil-documents.html) lists an original birth certificate or certified copy among civil documents and ties translation requirements to document language and country guidance. [UK visitor guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/visitor-visa-guide-to-supporting-documents/guide-to-supporting-documents-visiting-the-uk) gives a birth certificate as an example of evidence of a child’s relationship to a parent or guardian in a specific context, and requires a full independently verifiable translation for submitted documents outside English or Welsh. [Canadian immigration guidance](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=018) explains translation-related documents for supporting records outside English or French unless it instructs otherwise.

These are scoped examples, not a general birth-certificate checklist and not evidence that Globalize is approved by any recipient. Before ordering, read current instructions for the exact process, including document type, origin, language, and any additional step. Ask the recipient if a requirement is unclear.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- sending an image that hides a certificate edge or stamp;
- confusing a birth certificate with a family register or another civil record;
- changing a name spelling or date without reference to the source;
- assuming a scan is always sufficient for production or submission;
- treating a translation stamp as a substitute for a separately requested authentication;
- relying on someone else’s experience or outdated instructions for a different process;
- using a ready-made translated template that could be mistaken for an official form;
- expecting translation alone to secure an application, establish nationality, or prove a family relationship.

## Frequently asked questions

### What information is needed to translate a birth certificate?

Send a complete legible copy, issuing country and authority, source and target languages, recipient, purpose, requested deadline, and any required name spelling or formatting instructions.

### Do I need to send the original birth certificate?

An initial assessment can begin with a clear copy. Whether an original is needed for production or submission depends on the document, recipient instructions, and delivery process.

### Is a birth certificate the same document as a family register?

No. They have different titles and may contain different records. Send the document’s printed title and purpose so the enquiry can be routed correctly.

### Does translation guarantee that an embassy or university will accept the certificate?

No. Requirements vary by recipient and process, and the receiving organization makes the acceptance decision. Send its current instructions so the translation scope can be assessed without assuming acceptance.
