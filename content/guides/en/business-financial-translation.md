# Business and Financial Translation Services

**Short answer:** Globalize lists financial and business translation among its services. To request an assessment, describe the material, source and target languages, intended reader, and any terminology or formatting requirements. The specific scope must be reviewed for each request; translation does not validate figures, forecasts, accounting treatment, legal compliance, or a commercial outcome.

## Prepare a business translation enquiry

Business and financial files can contain organization-specific terminology, tables, figures, charts, and abbreviations. Share the context needed to understand the document and identify the intended use. If a term has an approved translation, provide the glossary or reference material rather than expecting the translator to infer a company’s internal usage.

Include:

- Document or content type and approximate volume
- Source and target languages
- Subject area and intended reader
- Whether tables, charts, or layout need to be preserved
- Any glossary, style guide, prior translation, or terminology preferences
- Recipient instructions and requested timing, if applicable

These are scoping details, not a commitment that every financial subdomain, file format, or layout service is available.

## Keep translation separate from professional advice

Translation transfers the source content into another language. It does not audit or certify financial figures, assess an investment, prepare tax or accounting advice, verify a forecast, or determine whether a document satisfies a bank, regulator, court, government office, or other recipient. Ask the relevant professional or recipient about those decisions.

Legal contracts and other documents whose primary purpose is legal should be scoped through the relevant legal-translation route. Official company records should be routed according to the document-specific service rather than assumed to be covered by this general business page. Academic research belongs to its own service intent.

## Make tables and figures reviewable

For documents containing financial information, preserve the relationship between labels, periods, currencies, units, and values. A table can be linguistically correct yet difficult to compare if a heading or footnote is separated from the figure it qualifies. Identify whether the source uses decimal or thousands separators, and provide any organization-specific terminology consistently across related files.

| Source element | Check against the source after translation |
|---|---|
| Reporting period | Start/end dates and whether the figure is monthly, annual, or cumulative |
| Currency and unit | Currency code/symbol, scale, and unit labels |
| Table headings | Row/column labels, notes, and references to schedules |
| Identifiers | Company names, account labels, invoice numbers, and legal entity names |
| Assumptions | Forecast notes or scenario labels are carried over as written |

This is a language and document-completeness check, not an audit. If the source appears internally inconsistent, do not correct a number or accounting treatment during translation. Mark the location and obtain clarification from the document owner or qualified financial adviser. When related files use different spellings or terms, identify the preferred reference and ask whether consistency or faithful reproduction of each source takes priority.

## Review business and financial source files before translation

A useful assessment begins with a readable, complete source set. Keep related schedules, appendices, footnotes, and referenced tables together where they explain a figure or term. Identify the version to translate and mark superseded files clearly. If a spreadsheet contains formulas, hidden sheets, comments, or linked workbooks, say whether those elements are part of the requested scope; a rendered PDF may not show them. This avoids treating a partial export as the complete record.

When several documents belong to one transaction or reporting period, provide a short file list and identify the relationship among them. A board resolution, financial statement, explanatory note, and bank form may use the same entity name differently. The translator should preserve the source and flag inconsistencies for the document owner rather than silently harmonize legal names, dates, amounts, or accounting labels.

| Source feature | Information that helps define the assignment |
|---|---|
| Multiple currencies or units | State whether symbols, codes, and scale labels must follow a supplied style guide |
| Financial tables | Identify whether editable tables or a visual reproduction is needed |
| Abbreviations | Supply an approved glossary or explain organization-specific meanings |
| Comparative periods | Keep each period and its notes connected to the correct figures |
| Illegible or conflicting entries | Mark the location and provide a clearer source or written instruction |

## Coordinate review and delivery expectations

Before work begins, agree which files are included, the target language, intended recipient, format, and any formatting constraints. If the recipient has a checklist, include the relevant part with the request. A translation can preserve the content and structure supplied, but it cannot determine whether a bank, investor, regulator, or court will accept the document for a particular purpose.

At review, compare names, dates, currencies, decimal separators, percentages, table headings, references, and footnotes against the source. Check that a negative value, approximation, qualifier, or reporting period has not changed. If a source contains an apparent error, the appropriate response is to flag it and request direction; correcting it without authorization would change the record.

For recurring reports, provide prior approved terminology when available. A glossary can make repeated entity names and accounting terms consistent, but it should not override a different term in a source document without an agreed rule. For sensitive material, use the file-transfer and confidentiality arrangements confirmed for the specific project. Do not send account passwords or access credentials as translation instructions.

## Keep related records and terminology aligned

Where several files use the same company, account, project, or reporting period, provide a naming reference and identify which source controls if values or labels differ. Keep notes that qualify a number beside the relevant table, and retain the distinction between actual, estimated, forecast, and comparative figures. If the target reader expects a particular date, currency, or number format, provide that instruction in writing. Formatting changes must not make two different amounts appear equivalent or detach a note from the value it explains.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does translation verify financial figures or forecasts?

No. Translation conveys the source content; it does not audit, validate, or certify the figures, assumptions, or forecast.

### Can I request translation of a feasibility study?

The current approved service record does not confirm feasibility studies as a named deliverable. Include the document type and scope in your enquiry for assessment; do not assume availability until it is confirmed for the request.

### Does this service include legal or tax advice?

No. Translation is not legal, tax, accounting, investment, or regulatory advice. Consult a qualified professional or the recipient for those questions.

### What should I include in a business translation request?

Provide the file type, source and target languages, subject, intended reader, approximate volume, any terminology or formatting requirements, and the requested timing. Avoid sending account passwords or sensitive records in an initial enquiry.
